Far from home

Where were the Victoria Crosses won, where were the men who won them born, and how far apart were the two? I matched all {{counts.recipients|int}} recipients to their birthplaces and located every place of action I could.

The Victoria Cross is Britain's highest award for gallantry in the face of the enemy. Since 1856 it has been given {{counts.awards|int}} times, for acts at a Baltic island fortress and on Crimean hillsides, at Lucknow and Delhi, Rorke's Drift and Colenso, Gallipoli and the Somme, Burma, Korea and Helmand. {{counts.posthumous|int}} of the recipients are marked in the list as posthumous awards, and many more died later in the same wars. Behind each award is one person and two places: where they were born and where they did the thing the citation describes.

I wanted to see both places on one map. I took the Wikipedia lists of recipients as the master list, matched each person to their birthplace on Wikidata, and located the place of action wherever the lists or Wikidata gave one. Birthplaces are almost complete: {{counts.birth_coords|int}} of {{counts.recipients|int}} ({{counts.share_birth|pct0}}). Places of action are not. I could pin down {{counts.place_precise|int}} of the {{counts.awards|int}} awards ({{counts.share_place_precise|pct0}}) to a named place, so the map of deeds is a sample, and I say below which way it leans.

Three things stand out. Ireland supplied a remarkable share of the early awards. The First World War dominates everything, and most of its British recipients won their crosses a few hundred kilometres from home. And as the empire's armies changed, so did the birthplaces: by the Second World War, a quarter of recipients were born in Australia, Canada, New Zealand or southern Africa, and nearly a fifth in South Asia.

    04 · Places of actionWhere the crosses were won

    For {{counts.place_precise|int}} awards I have a named place of action that I could put on the map. Grouping places within {{hotspot_radius|raw}} km of each other, the busiest are the Ypres salient ({{hot.ypres.n|int}} located awards), the Somme ({{hot.somme.n|int}}), Lucknow ({{hot.lucknow.n|int}}), Sevastopol and Balaklava ({{hot.sevastopol.n|int}}) and Delhi ({{hot.delhi.n|int}}). Rorke's Drift and Isandlwana, close together in Zululand and both fought on 22 and 23 January 1879, account for {{hot.rorke.n|int}} located awards, and Gallipoli for {{hot.gallipoli.n|int}}.

    These counts are floors, not totals: the Western Front is under-counted most, because the lists that give a place of action cover only some of its recipients. To get a fuller picture of where the crosses were won, I combined the located places with the campaigns that happened in a single region (the Indian Mutiny in India, the Boer wars in southern Africa, and so on). That places {{counts.region_known|int}} of the {{counts.awards|int}} awards ({{counts.share_region|pct0}}) in a region. The other {{regions_ww_unknown|int}} are all world-war awards whose place I could not locate.

    05 · DistanceHow far from home

    For {{distance.n|int}} awards I know both the birthplace and the place of action precisely enough to measure the distance between them. Their median is {{distance.median|int}} km, but that sample leans heavily towards Irish-born and dominion recipients, because those are the lists that give a place. Reweighting it so that each combination of birthplace group and conflict counts as often as it does in the full list (which covers {{distance.adjusted_cover|pct0}} of all awards) gives a typical distance of about {{distance.adjusted_median|int}} km. On that reweighted basis about {{distance.adjusted_share_under_1000|pct0}} of deeds were done within 1,000 km of the recipient's birthplace and {{distance.adjusted_share_over_5000|pct0}} more than 5,000 km away.

    The spread is the story. The {{ww1_distance_by_birth.England.n|int}} English-born recipients in the First World War with a located place of action won their crosses a median of {{ww1_distance_by_birth.England.median|int}} km from where they were born: Flanders and Picardy are a day's travel from Kent. Australians in the same war fought a median of {{ww1_distance_by_birth.Australia.median|int}} km from home, and New Zealanders {{ww1_distance_by_birth.New Zealand.median|int}} km, close to the far side of the planet. The longest journey in the data is {{farthest.0.name|raw}}'s, from {{farthest.0.born|raw}} to {{farthest.0.place|raw}} in {{farthest.0.year|raw}}: {{farthest.0.km|int}} km. The shortest is {{nearest.0.name|raw}}'s, who won his cross in {{nearest.0.year|raw}} at {{nearest.0.place|raw}}, the place Wikidata gives as his birthplace.

    07 · LimitsWhat this can and cannot say

    The place of action is the weak link. The main Wikipedia lists give each recipient's name, rank, unit, campaign and date, but not the place. The national lists (Irish, Indian Army, Australian, Canadian, New Zealand and by nationality) do give a place, so recipients in those lists are much better covered than English, Scottish and Welsh soldiers of British regiments. Located awards are {{distance_subset_share.Ireland|pct0}} Irish-born, against {{all_share.Ireland|pct0}} in the full list, and {{distance_subset_share.England|pct0}} English-born, against {{all_share.England|pct0}}. The reweighting in section 05 corrects for the mix of birthplaces and conflicts, but not for anything else that might differ between the people in those lists and the rest.

    Places can be coarse. Many places in the lists are small villages, ridges or woods. Where the list linked a Wikipedia article I used that article's coordinates; otherwise I looked the name up in GeoNames, in the stated country, and kept it only if it was unambiguous. A place like "Sevastopol" stands for a siege that lasted a year. Places that could only be resolved to a whole country or large region are left off the map and out of the distances.

    Birthplaces come from Wikidata and inherit its errors. A few are only a country ("Nepal"); those are left out of distances. Counties are today's, not those of the time. And the birthplace is not always home: many recipients grew up, enlisted or emigrated far from where they were born.

    No per-head rates. A fair comparison of counties or countries would divide by the number of men of military age at the time. I did not have historical populations at county level, so I give counts and say so.

    How I did this

    Master list. I parsed the three Wikipedia tables "List of Victoria Cross recipients (A to F)", "(G to M)" and "(N to Z)": {{counts.recipients|int}} named recipients. Three people won the cross twice (Noel Chavasse, Arthur Martin-Leake and Charles Upham), giving {{counts.awards|int}} awards. The Unknown Soldier of the United States, who was given the cross symbolically in 1921, is not in these tables, nor are the later awards of the separate Victoria Cross for Australia and Victoria Cross for New Zealand.

    Birthplaces. I joined the list to Wikidata through each recipient's English Wikipedia article ({{counts.wikidata_matched|int}} of {{counts.recipients|int}} matched) and took the place of birth and its coordinates. I placed each birthplace in a country with Natural Earth boundaries, and births in Great Britain in a county with ONS boundaries.

    Places of action. {{geocoding.rows|int}} rows of the six national lists give a place. I located {{geocoding.precise|int}} of them to a named place: through the Wikipedia article the list links to (checked to lie in the stated country), an unlinked name that is exactly the title of a linked article in that country, a unique GeoNames town of 1,000 people or more in that country, or a Wikidata battle named after the place. {{geocoding.region|int}} resolved only to a region and {{geocoding.unmatched|int}} ({{geocoding.share_unmatched|pct0}}) not at all; the file of every lookup is published with the data. I added {{counts.place_from_wikidata|int}} awards whose Wikidata record names exactly one specific battle with coordinates and a matching year.

    Distance. Great-circle distance between the two points. The adjusted median weights each located award by the number of awards in its birthplace group and conflict period in the full list, divided by the number located.

    Corrections. One row in the list gives the Indian Mutiny for William Knox Leet, whose deed was at Hlobane in the Anglo-Zulu War on 28 March 1879; I use the Anglo-Zulu War. Where a hidden sort date disagreed with the visible date, I used the visible year. The list links both John Williamses to one article; I gave the First World War recipient his own.
